## Summary

This Training Guide outlines the Hanson Corporate Quality Assurance Program (CQAP), describes the structure of the quality management system, and defines the roles and responsibilities for implementing and training in the CQAP.

## Details

### 1.0 Purpose

#### Description

- Provides a reference for the Hanson CQAP training materials for Practice Leads and Discipline Managers.

### 2.0 Scope

#### Description

- Covers the basic requirements and procedures of the CQAP, focusing on Tier I (Quality Manual) and Tier II (Quality Procedures); Tier III materials are briefly addressed.

### 3.0 What is a Quality Management System?

#### Definition

- A comprehensive system ensuring consistent achievement of quality and expected deliverables.

#### Objectives

1. Produce deliverables correctly on the first attempt.
2. Document planning, training, performance, verification, and corrective actions.

### 4.0 What is Hanson’s Total Quality Management System?

#### Philosophy

- Client awareness, perception, and brand
- Employee involvement
- Process improvement
- Achievement of quality

### 5.0 CQAP – Development, Structure and Applicability

#### Development

- Originated in the late 1980s from a client requirement for a formal QA program.

#### Structure

1. Tier I: Quality Manual ([[QUAL4000 - Quality Manual]])
2. Tier II: Quality Procedures (QPs)
3. Tier III: Standard practices, project documents, and forms

#### Applicability

- Standard for all Hanson projects; deviations only by client mandate, with project‑specific QA plans as needed.

### 6.0 Details on the Tier I Basic Requirements

#### Requirements Overview

1. QA Program overview (plan, execute, document)
2. Responsibilities, roles and authority
3. Planning related requirements
4. Training related requirements
5. Performance related requirements
6. Verification related requirements
7. Control of nonconformance and corrective/preventive actions
8. Role of discipline managers, quality staff, and training coordinators

### 7.0 Planning Related Requirements

#### Key Features

1. Establish responsibilities, roles and authority for QA implementation.
2. Use approved procedures and documents for project planning.
3. Define procurement, document control, and record‑keeping requirements.

### 8.0 Training Related Requirements

#### Key Features

- Train personnel on applicable Tier I, II and III documents.
- Assign Discipline Managers as CQAP trainers.
- Document all training activities and records.

### 9.0 Performance Related Requirements

#### Key Features

1. Ensure procured items and services meet specified requirements.
2. Pre‑approve subconsultants and verify equipment calibration.
3. Implement design control with independent verification.

### 10.0 Verification Related Requirements

#### Key Features

1. Conduct inspections and tests per defined methods by independent personnel.
2. Perform audits and surveillances using written procedures and checklists.
3. Document findings and ensure follow‑up actions.

### 11.0 Control of Nonconformance and Corrective/Preventive Actions

#### Key Features

- Identify and document nonconformances.
- Implement, record, and verify corrective or preventive actions.
- Mark or void nonconforming items to prevent unintended use.

### 12.0 Role of the Discipline Manager, Quality Staff and Talent Development Coordinator

#### Responsibilities

1. Discipline Managers provide indoctrination and training on CQAP requirements.
2. QA staff support audits, training, and program implementation.
3. Talent Development Coordinators assign training modules and maintain training records.
